# Expert A — Meal-planning behaviour & UX (academic & official)
Method: 11 web searches, 6 page fetches. Several primary pages (PubMed, PMC) were blocked; most claims rest on titles, abstracts and secondary pages. No claim is "verified"; CORROBORATED is the ceiling. Two-source rule applied: a claim with one source is ALLEGED. I inform; I do not recommend.

## 1. Planning vs diet quality, home cooking, waste, cost
- **Meal planning is associated with better diet quality and variety, and lower obesity odds, in French adults** (NutriNet-Santé, cross-sectional, large sample; Ducrot et al. 2017). Evidence is from the title and abstract list only; the paper could not be opened. Association only. CREDIBLE, since a second independent French study was not found. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28153017/ ; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5288891/ (same study).
- **Frequent home dinner cooking goes with fewer kJ, less sugar/fat and fewer fast-food meals** (NHANES n=9,569, cross-sectional; Wolfson & Bleich 2015). No link with body weight; the diet-index reliability was weak (α=0.36). https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/public-health-nutrition/article/is-cooking-at-home-associated-with-better-diet-quality-or-weightloss-intention/B2C8C168FFA377DD2880A217DB6AF26F . Together with Ducrot, the direction is consistent: CORROBORATED as association, not as causation. Cooking-intervention reviews exist but I did not read them (https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/handle/10919/111834): UNKNOWN for causal effect.
- **Waste:** modelling (not empirical) says package-size-aware weekly plans can reach near-zero waste (de Leeuw et al. 2024, https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/662912bc2b3930d7cdc09e8c). Modelling only: ALLEGED as a real-world effect size. No randomised trial of a planning app on waste was found: UNKNOWN.
- **France (ADEME):** about 30 kg of edible food wasted per person per year (about one meal a week), roughly 100 EUR per person. Cited causes: unplanned shopping without checking stock, multi-buy offers, forgotten items, oversized portions. https://agirpourlatransition.ademe.fr/particuliers/conso/alimentation/pourquoi-gaspillons-autant-nourriture . Corroborated in direction by the Australian study below. CREDIBLE for the figures (one official source read).
- **Cost:** no robust evidence found that planning lowers the food bill beyond waste reduction (the ADEME 100 EUR is the waste, not a planning effect). UNKNOWN.

## 2. Inventory, horizon, leftovers
- **Check household numbers before cooking, use-it-up shelf, weekly "cook what is going off" meal, plan 3-4 days ahead, written list.** A University of Technology Sydney / OzHarvest-based study ranked leftovers, a use-it-up shelf and portioning as top, high-impact and easy; the shopping list and 3-4 day plan came after. Mental effort was the commonest barrier; few households did these. https://phys.org/news/2021-09-food-home-evidence-based-ways.pdf . ADEME independently advises inventory before shopping, front-of-fridge placement for items to eat soon, and recycling leftovers (same URL as above). Two independent sources, but both are advice or surveys, not trials. CORROBORATED as the recommended practice; effectiveness size UNKNOWN.
- **Horizon:** the 3-4 day suggestion (Australia) differs from ADEME's "if possible, weekly menus" and from the charter's roughly 2 weeks. A 2-week horizon has no direct evidence either way: UNKNOWN. A short horizon is easier to keep; a longer one carries more drift risk (inference, not a finding).
- **Planning fallacy:** I found no meal-specific study; general planning-fallacy literature was not searched. UNKNOWN.

## 3. What makes change stick
- **Habit timing:** average 66 days to peak automaticity, range 18 to an extrapolated 254 days. One missed day did little harm; many missed repetitions lowered the final level (Lally et al. 2010, self-report, n small, healthy-behaviour tasks). https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/how-form-habit ; https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/does-it-really-take-66-days-form-habit-we-asked-expert-dr-pippa-lally . The authors and Surrey caution that "66 days" is not a rule. CORROBORATED (same study relayed; treat as CREDIBLE for rigour). Implication for the design: occasional lapses are tolerable.
- **Friction/mental effort:** the Australian study named effort as the main barrier (above). CREDIBLE.
- **Variety:** associated with diet quality in Ducrot (above). Mechanisms (sensory-specific satiety) were not checked. CREDIBLE.
- **App attrition:** dietary self-monitoring apps show heavy drop-off, and sustained use predicts outcomes (narrative review, photo-app cohort). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4004142/ ; https://nutrition-evidence.com/article/305508/defining-adherence-to-dietary-self-monitoring-using-a-mobile-app-a-narrative-review . Only titles and snippets read: CREDIBLE for "attrition is common", exact rates UNKNOWN.

## 4. Risks
- **Tracking and disordered eating:** a systematic review of 38 studies links diet/fitness-app use to more disordered-eating symptoms in young adults, with some users reporting greater awareness (Flinders, 2025). https://news.flinders.edu.au/blog/2025/02/22/fitness-apps-fuelling-disordered-eating/ . A MyFitnessPal study in eating-disorder patients also reports frequent calorie-tracker use (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28843591/, page not readable; title only). A study in adults seeking obesity treatment found no adverse effect of self-monitoring (https://www.aminer.org/pub/5c0f83fdda562944ac8d9f86/self-monitoring-has-no-adverse-effect-on-disordered-eating-in-adults-seeking). **DISAGREEMENT / CONTESTED:** risk concentrates in calorie- and weight-focused tracking among people already weight-concerned; it is not shown for non-quantified planning. Association designs; causation UNKNOWN. Grade: CREDIBLE.
- **Abandonment:** effort and rigid schedules are plausible causes, but I found no trial isolating them. UNKNOWN.

## Blind spots
Academic literature is dominated by US/Australian cross-sectional data, tests few planning apps, and has no usability data for a single-user offline planner. User reviews of existing apps (the field expert's base) are not covered here.
