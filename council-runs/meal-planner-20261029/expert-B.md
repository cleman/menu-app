# Expert B — Meal planning: practice & field
Method: 8 searches + 11 page fetches (pages opened, but reviews are App Store samples / forum threads, not systematic). Ceiling: CORROBORATED. I inform; I do not recommend. Many "sources" are vendor blogs (leaning noted).

## 1. Praised patterns
- **Recipe-calendar → auto-generated list that saves time and prevents forgotten items.** CORROBORATED. Plan to Eat reviews (appshunter.io/ios/app/1215348056/reviews); Mealime reviews (appshunter.io/ios/app/1079999103/reviews/13). Both are iOS review samples.
- **Import recipes from web pages + tags/categories.** CREDIBLE. Plan to Eat reviews (above). The only second source is a vendor comparison (plantoeat.com/?p=38013), so it is not independent. Import bugs are also reported: fractions and apostrophes get mangled (same Plan to Eat review page). ALLEGED, single reviewer.
- **List grouped by aisle/section, shared with household.** CREDIBLE. Plan to Eat reviews; Paprika's customizable aisles (plantoeat.com/?p=38013, competitor-biased).
- **Free tier that "actually functions".** ALLEGED (one Mealime review).
- Drag-drop week view, pantry-staples exclusion: no usable evidence found in this pass. UNKNOWN.

## 2. Abandonment / friction
- **Rigidity.** Mealime plans cannot be edited; removing a recipe means starting a new plan (Mealime reviews). Plan to Eat's one-recipe-per-night model is criticised as causing waste (Plan to Eat reviews). Two different apps and two reviewers, but a thin sample: CREDIBLE.
- **Subscription/paywall.** Plan to Eat ~$55/yr called expensive (Plan to Eat reviews); Mealime "incessantly" pushes upgrades (Mealime reviews). Paprika charges per device with no trial (plantoeat.com/?p=38013, competitor). CREDIBLE.
- **Account walls:** UNKNOWN. Mealime reviews do not mention them, and nothing else was found.
- **Data-entry burden / many taps.** Paprika: "most actions take several steps", ingredients do not carry over from the plan to the list automatically (plantoeat.com/?p=38013, competitor-biased, one source): ALLEGED.
- Samsung Food: unitQ gives score 69 "fair", but the page has no complaint text (unitq.com/unitq-scorecards/whisk/). UNKNOWN.

## 3. Home stock in practice
- **Exact-quantity inventory is abandoned because upkeep costs more than it saves.** CORROBORATED, three independent origins:
  - A Grocy user's failure write-up: effort outweighs benefit, unit and pack-size master data, half an evening per recipe, household buy-in (galaxus.ch/en/page/how-i-want-to-digitise-the-contents-of-my-fridge-and-fail-to-do-so-39565).
  - r/selfhosted Grocy thread: "Keeping track of the exact amount of everything is too cumbersome"; one user stalled after a month and the stock went stale (lr.us.psf.lt/r/selfhosted/comments/1dt5mdq/people_who_use_grocy_how_was_it_getting_your).
  - Plan to Eat discontinued its Pantry feature: wrong stock hid needed items, causing extra trips; "typing is harder than tapping"; it does not know your kitchen (learn.plantoeat.com/doc/a-digital-pantry-inventory-does-it-really-help; vendor, but a first-hand product decision).
- **What gets used instead:**
  - Look in the cupboards before shopping and tick items off the list ("only way to get a 100% accurate list"). The Plan to Eat doc and the Galaxus author converge: "moving used packs into the shopping list" (CORROBORATED, two origins).
  - Track only slow-moving or bulk items and the freezer (Grocy thread, one user). ALLEGED.
  - Mirepoix (small open-source app): an "Inventory Check" pass before shopping, re-asking confirmed staples at growing intervals and sooner after a surprise run-out (chris-biagini.github.io/mirepoix/groceries/learning). ALLEGED, single, niche source.
  - Shopping-list apps (AnyList, Bring!, Keep, KitchenOwl) win over inventory apps in that thread, with the Galaxus author's Bring! as a second origin: CREDIBLE.
- Approximate levels (full/half/empty) vs exact quantities: no direct evidence either way. UNKNOWN. Unit friction (onions, herbs, oil unquantifiable) in the Galaxus piece is indirect support for coarse levels only.
- Auto-deduction when cooking: no field evidence of it holding up; Galaxus notes weighing while cooking is impractical. ALLEGED.
- KitchenOwl pantry: only host/directory pages surfaced (linuxlinks.com/kitchenowl, hostinger); no user evidence. UNKNOWN.

## 4. How people really plan
- Wide variety, often hybrid: weekly Friday session picking 3-4 meals; 4-week rotating template; monthly around payday; category templates (meat/fish/veg/pasta/"easy"); carb rotation; lolly-stick draw; daily decide-and-buy; checking fridge/freezer first; batch-cook for freezer (mumsnet.com/talk/food_and_recipes/5158967-how-do-you-plan-your-weekly-meals; one UK forum thread). CREDIBLE for variety, not for frequencies.
- **Plan without fixed days:** Plan to Eat itself documents "add 3-4 recipes, shop from the list, pick each day by mood/energy" and an ingredients-only plan (plantoeat.com/blog/2025/02/meal-planning-that-fits-your-life-3-alternative-strategies; vendor). Mumsnet's template planners and daily deciders are an independent echo. CREDIBLE.
- 2-weekly cycles: only a monthly/4-week mention; no direct 2-week evidence. UNKNOWN.
- One-handed/kitchen use: no usable evidence found. Paprika complaint (buttons at the bottom led to tapping Cancel; cooking progress lost on leaving the recipe) is the nearest, single and competitor-biased: ALLEGED.

## Disagreements / tensions
- Plan to Eat advocates cupboard checks over pantry software; Paprika and Grocy keep inventory features. Users in the Grocy thread split: scale back vs. automate with barcode scanners and Home Assistant.
- Mealime defends locked plans as waste reduction; its reviewers find them rigid.

## Blind spots
Reviews skew iOS/US and English; no Play Store or French-market apps (Jow, Frigo Magic) were sampled; people who quit silently leave no reviews; vendor blogs lean commercial; self-hosters are not typical users.
